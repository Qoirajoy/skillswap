SkillSwap
One-Sentence Summary
SkillSwap is a university-wide platform that helps students find other students with the skills they need for academic, personal, or extracurricular tasks while giving them opportunities to offer their own skills in return.
The Problem
College students regularly need help with things they don't know how to do themselves, from improving a presentation or analyzing data to designing a poster or finding a collaborator for a larger project.
Students often find help by asking friends, classmates, or people in their major. This limits them to the networks they already have and makes it difficult to discover students elsewhere in the university who have the skills they need.
At the same time, students have skills they could offer to others but may not know who needs them.
SkillSwap creates a university-wide network where students can both request help and offer help. Because students can discover people across different colleges and schools, they can find expertise outside their immediate academic community.
A student might need help with Excel while offering Python help, while another student might need Python and offer Excel. SkillSwap can identify these complementary needs and connect students who may be able to help each other.
Skills are self-reported in the initial version. Students can provide their proficiency level and relevant experience so potential collaborators have context when deciding whether someone is a good fit.
User Types
•	Registered students — every student can create a profile, list skills they can offer, identify skills they want to learn, request help, offer help, and connect with other students.
•	There are no separate "helper" and "requester" roles. A student can be both at the same time.
Core Features
•	Create and manage a profile with college, major, skills, interests, proficiency levels, and relevant experience.
•	Post help requests describing something they need help with and the skills that would be useful.
•	Post skill offers describing skills they are willing to help others with.
•	Discover students, requests, and offers by skill, college, and other relevant filters.
•	Find potential matches and express interest in helping or exchanging skills with another student.
What Users See
Screen 1: Discover
A university-wide feed and search experience where students can discover help requests, skill offers, and other students.
Students can search for skills such as Python, Excel, graphic design, public speaking, video editing, or presentation design.
They can also filter by college or school, allowing them to find expertise both within their own college and across the university.
Screen 2: My Profile
A student's profile showing:
•	Name
•	College
•	Major
•	Year
•	Bio
•	Skills they can offer
•	Skills they want to learn
•	Relevant experience
Students can update their skills and profile information as their interests and experience change.
Screen 3: Requests & Offers
Students can create and manage two types of posts:
Help Requests
"I need someone to help me improve my PowerPoint presentation."
Skill Offers
"I can help students with Python and basic data analysis."
Each post includes the relevant skill, description, and status.
Screen 4: Matches
A page showing potential connections based on complementary needs and offers.
For example:
Potential SkillSwap
Maya needs Excel and offers Python.
Alex needs Python and offers Excel.
You may be able to help each other.
Students can review each other's profiles and express interest in connecting.
Example Scenario
Maya is a Business student who needs help improving a presentation for a class. She knows the material well but isn't confident with presentation design.
She creates a help request:
Need help with: Presentation Design
"I have a 10-minute class presentation next week. I have finished my research but would like help making my slides more visually engaging."
Maya also lists:
I can help with: Python
"I have experience using Python for basic data analysis."
SkillSwap shows Maya students across the university who offer presentation design.
She discovers Alex, an Engineering student who lists presentation design as a skill and has experience creating presentations for student organizations.
At the same time, Alex has a help request for Python because he needs help with a data-analysis project.
SkillSwap identifies a potential mutual match:
Maya needs Presentation Design → Alex offers Presentation Design
Alex needs Python → Maya offers Python
Maya and Alex can view each other's profiles and express interest in connecting.
The same platform can support a quick request for help with a presentation, a one-time skill exchange, or a larger academic or personal project.
